Observation~\ref{prop:reduction} shields security from filter faults, but not maximality, and it
rests on stores returning the metadata they stored (A2). A filter that admits too much shows up
online as violations; one that admits too little, or a store that alters metadata, does not. We
therefore test every store translation and every authorizer offline against
Equation~\ref{eq:sys-in}: differential testing in the line of the logic-bug oracles for database
engines~\cite{rigger2020testing}, with property-based generation~\cite{maciver2019hypothesis}, applied to
metadata-filter semantics across vector stores, for which we found no such
oracle~\cite{xie2025toward,wang2025towards}.

\paragraph{The kit.}
From a seed, the kit draws small corpora with confusable values (\texttt{1} and \texttt{"1"}; \texttt{"a"}
and \texttt{"A"}; \'e as one code point or two), missing and dotted fields, nested And and Or, empty In
filters and subjects in 300 extra groups. It checks searches against $E(\varphi)$: no document outside
$E(\varphi)$, $\min(k,|E(\varphi)|)$ of them, and text and metadata identical to what was stored. $k$ equals the
corpus size for half of the filtered searches, so that a false denial cannot hide outside the top $k$. Stores whose
index is approximate even on these corpora (Cassandra, and Vespa and Redis with HNSW) run in a sound-only mode. For
authorizers it requires $\mathrm{check}$ to agree with $m \models \varphi_s$ on every hit, which shows consistency, not
a correct policy. Its own suite plants 18 faulty backends and six faulty authorizers that it must reject
(Appendix~\ref{app:conformance}).

\paragraph{The campaign.}
We ran the kit with fixed versions, seeds and counts against 13 stores and 14 engine targets (OPA, OpenFGA, SpiceDB,
Cerbos, Cedar and Casbin, each with two policies, and Ory Keto), beside controls. Pinecone needs an API key we did not
have, so its adapter is not covered. A run is one (target, seed, variant), of 20 seeds and nine or seven data variants,
and takes the worst outcome of its examples. The first campaign completed 4,315 runs: 3,612 passed, 84 were violations and 619 were errors.
Both controls passed every run, no target returned a hit outside the reference, and none altered stored metadata.
Of the violations, 39 came from the layer's Cerbos translator, which maps CEL's \texttt{in}, \texttt{==} and
\texttt{hasIntersection} to $\mathrm{In}$, which also matches a scalar, while Cerbos's check raises on a scalar and
denies: a type gap in the adapter, not a fault of Cerbos, which the project's review had found and the campaign
reproduced. The other 45 were Redis (26) and MongoDB (19), which cut a long term when they index it and look up the
whole term when they search. Of the errors, 479 were OpenFGA and SpiceDB refusing whole batches with HTTP~400, and
140 were stores' documented limits, which fail loudly and return nothing.

We changed the four adapters: OpenFGA and SpiceDB now refuse a subject they cannot represent before sending
anything, and Redis and MongoDB store and search a term above their limits as its SHA-256 digest. The second campaign, of
4,480 runs, had 3,822 passes, 39 violations, 479 refusals and 140 errors, as the diagnosis predicted. Redis and MongoDB
passed every long- and huge-string run, and in all 280 disagreeing Cerbos hits the filter gave the reference answer and
the check denied. The 479 refusals are 85.5\% of those two engines' 560 runs, and only 81 runs reached them with every
subject, so the campaign's evidence on them is thin; Section~\ref{sec:engines} tests them on data within their limits.
