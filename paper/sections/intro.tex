A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) assistant answers from the chunks of an index that are most
similar to the question~\cite{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}. In an organization those chunks come
from documents with different readers: a payroll file, a board deck, one customer's contract and a
public handbook can share one index. Similarity knows nothing of who may read what, so without
enforcement the chunk most similar to an employee's question may be one that employee may not open,
and the model will repeat it. OWASP lists this as LLM08, vector and embedding
weaknesses~\cite{owasp2025llm082025}, and revoked documents have stayed retrievable through a cached
index~\cite{roychowdhury2024confusedpilot}.

The remedy is old. Query modification adds the access predicate to the query before it
runs~\cite{stonebraker1974access}; multi-user full-text search showed that statistics computed over
unreadable documents reveal them~\cite{buttcher2005security}; enterprise search checked results late, in
growing blocks~\cite{bailey2006secure}; Zanzibar checks each result against a central authorization
service~\cite{pang2019zanzibar}. Managed RAG services combine these steps~\cite{aws2026access}, and recent
work builds permissions into the vector index itself~\cite{zhong2026honeybee,han2026dont}.

What is not known is whether the stack that implements the remedy is faithful. Three things stand
between an organization's policy and the chunks a model sees. \emph{First, the policy is compiled by
translators.} Filter APIs are code paths separate from check APIs, every store has its own filter
language, and we found no published guarantee that the paths agree. \emph{Second, the filter runs inside
an approximate index.} Permission filters are hard cases for filtered nearest-neighbor search: they are
can be selective, they take a disjunction over many groups, and organizational structure, not meaning,
decides which chunks they admit~\cite{patel2024acorn,lim2026revisiting}. \emph{Third, failures are
silent.} Under Truman semantics~\cite{rizvi2004extending} a subject who receives three chunks, or none,
cannot tell whether that is all it may read or all that the index found.

We test one enforcement layer as deployed, \sys{}, an open-source library that compiles an organization's
existing policy into filters for 14 vector stores, from seven authorization engines, re-checks every hit
with the authorizer and fails closed (Section~\ref{sec:system}), and we use it as an instrument. The
re-check rests security on a small trusted computing base around the check API
(Observation~\ref{prop:reduction}): a faulty filter or a lossy index can then cost recall but cannot
put a chunk outside the readable set into an answer. The open questions become how much is lost, where,
how fresh the check is, what the answers still reveal, and whether anyone can tell. Our answer is the
title, with its scope stated. By \emph{leak-free} we mean one thing: in every store and engine we
tested, no returned chunk lay outside the subject's readable set at the revision the re-check read.
That revision can be stale, and the release we evaluated exceeded its stated bound after an invalidation.
Other observables of an answer still revealed information about unreadable chunks. And retrieval quality
ranged from near perfect to missing most of what a subject may read, and the application could not see
the loss.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{A formulation} (Section~\ref{sec:problem}). We adapt the sound, secure and maximal
        criteria of Wang et al.~\cite{wang2007correctness} to top-$k$ retrieval under an external
        authorizer, add freshness, with a measured staleness $\delta$ per path, and count-honesty for the
        claim that an answer is complete, and state the trusted computing base.
  \item \emph{Verification of the enforcement path} (Sections~\ref{sec:conformance},
        \ref{sec:engines} and~\ref{sec:e2e}). Differential testing across 13 of the 14 stores and seven
        engines found two undocumented store behaviors with a correctness effect, two availability faults
        in the layer's adapters and documented limits that fail closed. At one revision, over 6.3 million
        user-document pairs, filters and checks never disagreed in the profiles that hold the model as
        intended. No chunk outside the readable set was returned in 1,050 fault episodes or in four runs of
        each of eleven end-to-end configurations ($6.8 \times 10^{-4}$ per question and
        $1.6 \times 10^{-2}$ per person, one-sided 95\%, over the generator's question distribution). A
        mutation control shows that the leak checks fail when the filter is broken on purpose and that
        the re-check contains the fault.
  \item \emph{An audit, diagnosis and repair of retrieval quality} (Sections~\ref{sec:quality}
        and~\ref{sec:repair}). Mean recall@10 at default or tuned settings ranged from 0.55 to 0.98 over eight
        stores searched approximately, under this layer's schema. Pre-registered experiments trace
        Qdrant's loss to an islanded permitted subgraph (0.656 in the shared layout, 0.952 with
        per-tenant graphs) and pgvector's to PostgreSQL's plan cache and two scan limits. A count flagged all
        2,657 truly short answers without a false alarm, and exact repair without a bound left none empty
        or short, but no count sees a full answer of the wrong chunks.
  \item \emph{Side channels of filtered retrieval} (Section~\ref{sec:side}). In a pre-registered
        distinguishing game, a subject who repeated a query could tell that 100 or more unreadable
        chunks had appeared near it from the widening post-filter's latency (paired AUC 0.80 to 0.98, except
        pgvector's 0.66 for an attacker reading 10\% of the background) and from BM25 and IDF scores (0.98
        to 1.00). Exact pre-filtering returned identical answers.
\end{itemize}

We do not propose an index, and we do not defend against prompt injection in readable content. The library,
the conformance kit, the pre-registrations and the raw outputs are released with the paper
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Appendix~\ref{app:map} maps every experiment to its versions and runs, and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} lists every hypothesis with its registration and verdict; labels such as P5 below
refer to that ledger.
