\paragraph{Limitations.}
Appendix~\ref{sec:limits-limits} states these in full. \emph{Leak-free} means that no returned chunk lay outside the
readable set at the revision the check read, under the stated trusted computing base. It does not mean fresh, nor that
unreadable chunks leave no trace, and the detection fields of Section~\ref{sec:repair} are observables we did not measure.
Every permission layout is generated, and the enterprise layout has 19 near-identical copies of each company document; Qdrant's recall depends on that
layout (Section~\ref{sec:qual-qdrant}), so these recall numbers should not be read as an estimate for an organization without such copies. Every
measurement passes through one layer, and some numbers are properties of its schema as much as of a store. No single version of the library was
evaluated throughout, and PostgreSQL and the embedding dimension differ across experiments (Appendix~\ref{app:map}). We did not measure native
enforcement (PostgreSQL row-level security, document-level security in Elasticsearch and OpenSearch), sweep the share of near-duplicate chunks or
rerun the evaluation on one release, and outside the 100\% selectivity cells we have no unfiltered baseline per store. Zero-leak results are upper
bounds over the generator's distribution; we apply no correction for multiple comparisons across cells. The system under test, its oracles, the
meta-tests that judge the oracles and the scripts that compute the verdicts share one source, so a misconception common to them would pass every check, a
common-mode risk that repeated runs cannot remove. Three things limit it, without removing it: the oracles were written without the library's filter code,
the vendor reproducers use only the vendors' clients, and we release the outputs so that others can recompute the verdicts.

\paragraph{Ethics and disclosure.}\label{sec:limits-ethics}
The author develops \sys{}, the layer under test, at \url{https://github.com/muzakkirhussain011/wardowl}, the repository that the pull-request numbers in
this paper refer to, and this evaluation found faults in that code as well as in others' (Table~\ref{tab:conf-findings}). No human subjects took part: people, documents, ACLs and canaries are synthetic, and every store and engine ran in containers or processes we
controlled. None of our findings is a chunk returned through the layer outside the readable set at the checked revision. They can still matter for
deployments that trust a pushed-down filter without a re-check, and Chroma's crash lets any user in enough groups stop an application that passes
their groups to Chroma (Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}). For the truncation in Redis and in MongoDB, Chroma's crash and the CEL semantics that the Cerbos
translator meets we wrote reports with minimal reproducers that do not need our library, and on 2026-10-30 sent those for Redis
(\url{https://github.com/RediSearch/RediSearch/issues/12106}), Chroma (\url{https://github.com/chroma-core/chroma/issues/7969},
\url{https://github.com/chroma-core/chroma/issues/7970}), Cerbos (\url{https://github.com/cerbos/cerbos/issues/3456}) and MongoDB (as documentation
feedback, which has no public link);
Table~\ref{tab:conf-faults} in Appendix~\ref{sec:conf-results} gives each report's status. SpiceDB's staleness beyond its documented bound, OPA's residual negation and pgvector's plan
choice have no report. No maintainer had replied to these reports by 2026-10-30.

\paragraph{Use of AI assistance.}\label{sec:limits-ai}
Under the author's direction, an AI coding agent produced most of the library's code, the conformance kit, the oracles, the harnesses and the analysis
scripts, drafted the pre-registrations, the evidence summaries and this text, and checked the references against primary records; the numbers audit and the
critical review cited here were run by separate instances of it. The author chose the research questions, took the decisions recorded with the
pre-registrations and reviews, and takes full responsibility for the content.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}\label{sec:limits-repro}
We release the library at the evaluated commits, the conformance kit, every harness script, the Kaggle notebooks and GitHub workflows that ran them, the
pre-registrations with their dated amendments, and the raw outputs with container image digests and seeds \tbd{archive DOI}. Compute: about 37 hours of Kaggle sessions (two T4 GPUs or four CPUs each) and more than 140 hours of GitHub-hosted runner jobs,
listed per experiment in Appendix~\ref{app:compute}; no experiment reported here ran on a local machine. Data and model licenses are in
Appendix~\ref{app:licenses}.
