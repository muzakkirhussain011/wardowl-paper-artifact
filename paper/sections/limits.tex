\paragraph{Limitations.}
Appendix~\ref{sec:limits-limits} states these in full. \emph{Leak-free} means that no returned chunk lay outside the
readable set at the revision the check read, under the stated trusted computing base. It does not mean fresh, nor that
unreadable chunks leave no trace, and the detection fields of Section~\ref{sec:repair} are observables we did not measure.
Every permission layout is generated, and the enterprise layout, with 19 near-identical copies of each company document, is a hard case for
graph indexes, so its recall numbers, Qdrant's especially, should not be read as an estimate for an organization without such copies. Every
measurement passes through one layer, and some numbers are properties of its schema as much as of a store. No single version of the library was
evaluated throughout, and PostgreSQL and the embedding dimension differ across experiments (Appendix~\ref{app:map}). We did not measure native
enforcement (PostgreSQL row-level security, document-level security in Elasticsearch and OpenSearch), sweep the share of near-duplicate chunks or
rerun the evaluation on one release, and outside the 100\% selectivity cells we have no unfiltered baseline per store. Zero-leak results are upper
bounds over the generator's distribution; we apply no correction for multiple comparisons across cells.

\paragraph{Ethics and disclosure.}\label{sec:limits-ethics}
No human subjects took part: people, documents, ACLs and canaries are synthetic, and every store and engine ran in containers or processes we
controlled. None of our findings is a chunk returned through the layer outside the readable set at the checked revision. They can still matter for
deployments that trust a pushed-down filter without a re-check, and Chroma's crash lets any user in enough groups stop an application that passes
their groups to Chroma (Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}). For the truncation in Redis and in MongoDB, Chroma's crash and the CEL semantics that the Cerbos
translator meets we prepared reports with minimal reproducers that do not need our library;
Table~\ref{tab:conf-faults} in Appendix~\ref{sec:conf-results} gives each report's status. SpiceDB's staleness beyond its documented bound, OPA's residual negation and pgvector's plan
choice have no report. \tbd{filing dates, links and vendor responses, once the reports are filed}

\paragraph{Use of AI assistance.}\label{sec:limits-ai}
An AI coding agent produced most of the artifacts of this work under the authors' direction: the library's code, the conformance kit and its
meta-tests, the independent oracles, the experiment harnesses and analysis scripts, the drafts of the pre-registrations, the evidence summaries of each
run, the checks of every reference against a primary record, and drafts of this text. Unverifiable reference fields were omitted rather than guessed. The
numbers audit and the critical review that this version answers were run by separate agent instances. The authors chose the research questions, took the
decisions recorded with the pre-registrations and reviews, and are responsible for every claim. \tbd{extent of the authors' own review of the code,
pre-registrations, evidence summaries and text} \tbd{Final human check of every reference before submission.} Because the system under test, the oracles
that judge it, the meta-tests that judge the oracles and the scripts that compute the verdicts come from one agent, a misconception shared among them would
pass every check, a common-mode risk to validity that repeated runs cannot remove. Three things limit it, without removing it: the oracles were written
without the library's filter code, the vendor reproducers use only the vendors' clients, and we release the outputs so that others can recompute the verdicts.
AI tools are not authors. This statement follows the venue's policy on large language models and arXiv's policy on generative AI.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}\label{sec:limits-repro}
We release the library at the evaluated commits, the conformance kit, every harness script, the Kaggle notebooks and GitHub workflows that ran them, the
pre-registrations with their dated amendments, and the raw outputs with container image digests and seeds \tbd{archive DOI}. For review, an anonymized mirror with
the package renamed is provided. Compute: about 35 hours of Kaggle sessions (two T4 GPUs or four CPUs each) and more than 140 hours of GitHub-hosted runner jobs,
listed per experiment in Appendix~\ref{app:compute}; no experiment reported here ran on a local machine. Data and model licenses are in
Appendix~\ref{app:licenses}.
