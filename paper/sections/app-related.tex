\section{Related work in full}\label{app:related}

This appendix gives the full account behind Section~\ref{sec:related}. Our statements that we found no prior work report searches, which are not exhaustive.

\paragraph{Enterprise search and document-level security.}
B\"{u}ttcher and Clarke gave the founding security model for multi-user full-text
search~\cite{buttcher2005security}. Filtering results ranked with global BM25 statistics lets any user
who can create files infer how many unreadable files contain a term (to within about 0.5\% from ranks
alone, with roughly ten binary-search queries); their fix, query integration, restricts every posting
list to the user's searchable set before ranking, at between 39\% faster and 17\% slower, and they
require that no aspect of the output, including its order, depend on unsearchable content. Bailey,
Hawking and Matson modeled late binding, whose cost grows with the results checked times the
per-check latency divided by the visibility density $\mathit{VD}$ (a 6.4\,ms off-server check limits
a one-second budget to 93 checked results), and fetched blocks of
$2\,\mathit{NR}/\mathit{VD}$ candidates until a page of $\mathit{NR}$ results
filled~\cite{bailey2006secure}. Google's Search Appliance team named early and late binding and
warned that ACLs copied into an index drift out of sync and expose
content~\cite{mangtani2006documentlevel}. Fagin et al.\ studied search over a corporate
intranet~\cite{fagin2003searching}, the setting in which these documents and their readers live. The
pre-filter we test is query integration applied to approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search, and
its widening post-filter is Bailey et al.'s loop; we claim neither. We use B\"{u}ttcher and Clarke's
consistency definition to frame side channels of filtered vector search. Their score channel has since been exploited and extended: Wang et al.'s STRESS attack discovers terms in other tenants' documents and counts documents holding a term, on shared indexes in Elasticsearch and on services such as GitHub, with rank-only variants and proposed countermeasures~\cite{wang2017sidechannel}, and Espiritu and Cash reach indexed terms and approximate text through document-level security in Elasticsearch and OpenSearch~\cite{espiritu2026plaintext}. The nearest peer-reviewed work to our side-channel measurement is therefore on keyword search and is stronger than ours there. We found no peer-reviewed measurement of these channels for filtered vector search, though one open-source project reports them in process~\cite{raghuram2026onewayglass}. A separate line protects the index from its server. Zerber bounds what an index reveals
about terms under statistical attacks, with limited trust in the central indexer~\cite{zerr2008zerber},
and Zerber+R adds top-$k$ ranking over such an index~\cite{zerr2009zerberr}; searchable encryption
protects keyword search~\cite{song2000practical,curtmola2006searchable}, and secure kNN protects
similarity search over encrypted vectors~\cite{wong2009secure}. Those works assume an untrusted
server; ours trusts the store's host (Section~\ref{sec:prob-threat}) and asks how one layer's
query path behaves.

\paragraph{Database access control.}
System R gave relational databases discretionary GRANT and REVOKE~\cite{griffiths1976authorization},
and Motro derived authorizations by algebraic manipulation of view
definitions~\cite{motro1989access}. Stonebraker and Wong enforced access rules by query modification,
conjoining the applicable rules' qualifications with the user's query~\cite{stonebraker1974access},
which is what a pushed-down permission filter does. Rizvi et al.\ separated Truman semantics, which
silently answer over the user's restricted view as Oracle's Virtual Private Database does, from
non-Truman semantics, which reject queries that authorized data cannot
answer~\cite{rizvi2004extending,oracle2026using}. Wang et al.\ defined sound, secure and maximum
answers, with security as indistinguishability across database states the policy cannot tell apart; they showed that the cell-level
disclosure control of Hippocratic databases~\cite{lefevre2004limiting} violates soundness and
the maximum property, noted that the straightforward extension of authorization-view query modification to queries with negation is not sound, and gave a sound and secure algorithm for cell-level policies, with the proof of one theorem left to a technical report~\cite{wang2007correctness}; Kabra et al.\ showed that functions evaluated before the authorization predicate reveal data through their values
and error messages~\cite{kabra2006redundancy}; and optimal security-aware query processing is
impossible for the full relational calculus~\cite{guarnieri2014optimal}. Sieve enforces large numbers
of fine-grained policies from a middleware above the database~\cite{pappachan2020sieve}. PostgreSQL
row-level security brings these lessons to production with default deny, leakproof functions and
documented covert channels through key checks~\cite{postgresql2026row}, and its timing has been attacked, first to test whether a value exists~\cite{dar2023rls,rasin2024vulnerability} and then to recover records~\cite{espiritu2026plaintext}. Of such native
enforcement we report only a side run of its recall and latency on another corpus (Appendix~\ref{sec:limits-native}), not its channels. The layer we test is query modification with Truman
semantics, and
its re-check deliberately keeps the redundancy that Kabra et al.\ remove, because its filter and its
check come from different code paths. This lineage gives criteria and, for cell-level policies under exact relational evaluation, a proved algorithm, not a general proof that rewriting is correct. We
adapt its soundness and maximality to top-$k$ retrieval and weaken its security (Section~\ref{sec:problem}), and test translators and approximate indexes, for which we found no such
proofs, where maximality can fail even when the predicate is right.

\paragraph{Authorization systems and policy-to-filter compilation.}
Zanzibar stores more than two trillion ACL tuples and answers millions of checks per second at p95
latency under 10\,ms. Answering a search query there requires ACL checks for all candidate results, often tens to hundreds of checks per set of results, and its Expand API lets clients build search indexes for access-controlled content; snapshot tokens prevent the ``new enemy''
problem of applying an old ACL to new content~\cite{pang2019zanzibar}. Its open-source descendants,
built on relationship-based access control~\cite{fong2011relationshipbaseda}, added reverse lookups
with caveats. OpenFGA recommends ListObjects for about 1,000 accessible objects and by default stops a
non-streaming call at 1,000 results or 3 seconds~\cite{openfga2026search,openfga2026openfga}; SpiceDB
recommends LookupResources below about 10,000 resources and otherwise a bulk-check loop pinned to one
revision~\cite{authzed2026protecting}, and CVE-2025-65111 is a case in which, for one schema shape before version 1.47.1, LookupResources missed results while its other APIs computed permissions correctly~\cite{nvd2025cve202565111}. A truncated allow-list is safe but silently loses recall.
Policy engines compile filters through engineering artifacts: OPA's partial evaluation, available since
2018, emits SQL conditions for a documented fragment of its
language~\cite{sandall2018write,opa2026evaluating,opa2026writing}, and Cerbos's query plans return
always-allowed, always-denied or a conditional syntax tree~\cite{cerbos2026cerbos}. The peer-reviewed
Cedar paper does not discuss filtering~\cite{cutler2024cedar}, and XACML 3.0 returns one decision per
request~\cite{oasis2013extensible}. Policies and engines themselves have been verified and tested:
Margrave checks properties of access-control policies and the impact of changing
them~\cite{fisler2005verification}, Zelkova reasons about AWS access policies with an SMT
solver~\cite{backes2018semanticbased}, mutation testing targets access-control
policies~\cite{martin2007fault}, and Cedar's production code is differentially tested against a model
with machine-checked proofs~\cite{disselkoen2024how}. These address the policy and its check path. The nearest result we found to a verified filter is the Lean theorem \texttt{reauthorize\_is\_sound} for Cedar's type-aware partial evaluator, in its public specification~\cite{cedar2026cedarspec}: re-authorizing a residual with concrete inputs equals direct authorization. It concerns residuals, not a translation into a store's filter language; we found no peer-reviewed report of it; and the layer's Cedar adapter does not use the evaluator (Appendix~\ref{sec:sys-contracts}). We
found no peer-reviewed semantics or correctness proof for any policy-to-filter translator. We propose
no authorizer or filter API. We measure, at one revision
token, how often each engine's filters disagree with its own check API on one generated model and when its list APIs
truncate (Section~\ref{sec:engines}), and rely on the re-check so that such faults cost recall, not
membership in the readable set.

\paragraph{Filtered ANN search and access-aware vector indexes.}
Graph indexes such as HNSW~\cite{malkov2020efficient} answer filtered queries by filtering before,
after or during traversal. With pass fraction $p$, a post-filter needs about $k/p$ candidates when passing items
are spread evenly and $O(n)$ when they lie far from the query~\cite{patel2024acorn}, and the collapse
is well measured: post-filtering stayed below 0.9 recall on LAION-1M even with positively correlated
filters~\cite{patel2024acorn}, Faiss-HNSW with post-filtering and ACORN often failed at 0.1\%
selectivity~\cite{li2025attribute}, and without a fallback ACORN-1's recall falls to 0.03--0.10 at
0.3\% pass fraction~\cite{kim2026racorn1}. pgvector filters after the index scan, so a filter matching
10\% of rows yields about four matches at the default search depth~\cite{pgvector2026pgvector}.
Index designs for attribute-filtered search include proximity graphs that fuse vectors and
attributes~\cite{wang2023efficient,wu2022hqann}, space partitions~\cite{gupta2023caps}, graphs that
navigate label sets~\cite{cai2024navigating}, workload-aware collections of
indexes~\cite{li2025sieve} and structures for range filters~\cite{zuo2024serf,xu2024irangegraph,engels2024approximate},
and systems combine vector and relational predicates in one engine~\cite{wei2020analyticdbv,zhang2023vbase};
all take the predicate as part of the query rather than compile it from an external policy.
Engines now fall back to exact scoring for restrictive
filters~\cite{aquino2024filtering,rodriguez2024how,trent2025filtered}; since version 3.5, OpenSearch's Faiss engine
does so when efficient-filtered search returns fewer than $k$ results while at least $k$ documents
pass, unless a setting disables it~\cite{opensearch2026efficient}. Filtered-DiskANN cites user privileges as motivation but
evaluates single-label filters~\cite{gollapudi2023filtereddiskann}; the NeurIPS'23 Big-ANN filtered
track uses conjunctions of one or two tags without analyzing correlation~\cite{simhadri2025results};
later benchmarks sweep selectivity down to 0.1\%~\cite{li2025attribute,iff2026benchmarking,shi2025filtered}
and control or measure query-filter correlation~\cite{zhang2026vecbench,amanbayev2026filtered,lu2026indepth};
and HCBGen shows that neither selectivity nor correlation alone is a stable proxy for
difficulty~\cite{lim2026revisiting}. None of those we read uses real ACLs, and neither do we. Permission-specific indexes are recent and
assume role-based access control. HoneyBee partitions by role structure, with up to 13.5$\times$ lower
latency than PostgreSQL row-level security at 1.24$\times$ the memory, on synthetic permissions and
with a cost model that ignores query-permission correlation~\cite{zhong2026honeybee}; Veda builds an
access-aware lattice over 641 to 757 role combinations~\cite{han2026dont}; and the SeQureDB '26 vision
paper finds on pgvector that pre-filtering gives the best recall and naive post-filtering near-zero
recall for selective policies~\cite{yalamarthi2026policyaware}. Curator indexes tenants without a
security model~\cite{jin2024curator}, and a recent preprint links per-tenant recall loss partly to the
local density of a tenant's points~\cite{das2026recallbounded}. We propose no index, and claim neither the first permission-aware vector search, nor the post-filter collapse, nor
alignment as a new axis, which we measure instead. We found no evaluation of per-query disjunctions
over hundreds or thousands of group labels, the shape of a directory-group ACL, and no audit of
production stores under such filters at documented, tuned and exact settings
(Sections~\ref{sec:quality} and~\ref{sec:repair}). HoneyBee and Veda are physical designs on which an
enforcement layer could run.

\paragraph{RAG privacy and agent identity.}
Anyone who can query a RAG system can drain its store. Composite prompts extract private retrieval
data, and re-ranking barely helps~\cite{zeng2024good}; prompt injection leaked the datastores of 25 of
25 custom GPTs with at most two queries each~\cite{qi2024follow}; interrogation attacks infer
membership with about 30 natural-looking queries~\cite{naseh2025riddle}; and in a survey's testbed, an
8-gram output guard cut verbatim copying to 0.0\% while an adaptive three-query attacker still
recovered 48.3\% of hidden facts~\cite{liu2026protection}. Such defenses are probabilistic and
model-side. Cross-tenant work reaches our premise: retrieval ranks by relevance, not authorization,
and attribute-based gating at retrieval removed cross-tenant leakage~\cite{arceo2026securing};
re-checking authorization at the graph-expansion boundary of hybrid RAG cut a retrieval-pivot risk of
0.95 to near zero~\cite{thornton2026retrieval}; Microsoft researchers call prompt sanitization and
output filtering fundamentally probabilistic~\cite{bhatt2025enterprise}; GPT-4.1 reaches only
$F_1 = 0.27$ on OrgAccess's hardest set, so prompts cannot carry permissions~\cite{sanyal2025orgaccess};
and OWASP's LLM08:2025 makes permission-aware vector and embedding stores its first prevention
measure~\cite{owasp2025llm082025}. PermLLM enforces access control in model parameters
instead~\cite{jayaraman2025permissioned}; ConfusedPilot showed revoked documents staying retrievable
through a cached index~\cite{roychowdhury2024confusedpilot}; and embedding
inversion~\cite{song2020information,li2023sentence,morris2023text,zhang2025universal} leaves storage
protection to the deployment. For agents, the confused deputy~\cite{hardy1988confused} has a
standards answer: RFC 8693's actor claim names the delegated agent while the subject's rights govern
access~\cite{jones2020oauth}, the MCP authorization specification requires audience-bound tokens and
forbids passing a client's token upstream~\cite{mcp2026model}, and MITRE ATLAS mitigation AML.M0027
says an agent should never hold permissions its user would not~\cite{mitre2026atlas}; yet 40.55\% of
7,973 live remote MCP servers exposed tools without authentication~\cite{zhou2026first}. This
literature shows why enforcement must be deterministic and precede generation, but does not test
whether an enforcement layer is faithful or what it costs in recall. Corpus
poisoning~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag} acts through content a reader may read, and information-flow
control for agents~\cite{costa2025securing,wu2024systemlevel} constrains where retrieved content may
flow after it is read; both are complementary to a retrieval guarantee. We adopt token-bound identity
rather than claim it, exclude extraction within the readable set and injection in permitted content
(Section~\ref{sec:prob-threat}), and take the timing channel that SpliceLeak made practical in RAG
infrastructure~\cite{sun2026agentassisted} as one motivation to measure side channels of filtered
retrieval.

\paragraph{Side channels and information flow.}
Noninterference requires that what a high-privilege user does has no effect on what a low-privilege
user observes~\cite{goguen1982security}. The distinguishing game of Section~\ref{sec:side-game} tests a
restricted form of it, over chosen observables and chosen changes to unreadable content. Timing has
long revealed hidden content on the web: load times revealed a browser's cache and so its
history~\cite{felten2000timing}, and the response times of search endpoints let a cross-site attacker
extract private data~\cite{gelernter2015crosssite}. Black-box mitigation delays outputs to a predicted
schedule~\cite{askarov2010predictive}, and language-based work combines such mitigation with
information-flow control~\cite{zhang2012languagebased}; the padding we measure is the simplest such
schedule, a fixed budget. We propose no mitigation beyond these. For keyword search the attacks cited under enterprise search above are stronger than our game, and their countermeasures, per-tenant indexes and document frequencies from public data, are the ones to read. What we add is a forced-choice measurement, through one layer, of how
much each enforcement path of filtered vector search reveals, and of what removing it costs.

\paragraph{Testing methodology.}
Database engines have mature oracles for logic bugs: Pivoted Query Synthesis found 121 bugs in
SQLite, MySQL and PostgreSQL~\cite{rigger2020testing}; NoREC and Ternary Logic Partitioning compare a
query with a non-optimizing or partitioned rewrite of itself~\cite{rigger2020detecting,rigger2020finding};
and later oracles reach query plans and graph databases~\cite{ba2023testing,ba2024keep,kamm2023testing}.
All descend from differential~\cite{mckeeman1998differential} and
metamorphic~\cite{chen2018metamorphic,segura2016survey} testing, often with property-based input
generation~\cite{claessen2000quickcheck,maciver2019hypothesis}. For the filter semantics of vector databases we found no such oracles:
a study of 1,671 bug-fix pull requests in 15 vector databases found 57.3\% of bugs to be functional
failures, with filter abnormality as a fault class, but does not mention differential or metamorphic
testing~\cite{xie2025toward}, and a testing roadmap lists oracle definition as an open
challenge and reports only simplified metamorphic cases~\cite{wang2025towards}. The Secret Sharer inserted canaries to estimate unintended
memorization~\cite{carlini2019secret}. In reporting, ANN work plots recall-throughput
frontiers~\cite{aumuller2020annbenchmarks}, yet the Big-ANN results paper and HoneyBee give no error
bars~\cite{simhadri2025results,zhong2026honeybee}, short of the practice recommended for ML
benchmarks~\cite{bouthillier2021accounting,agarwal2021deep}; fuzzing research has similar reporting
norms for repeated campaigns and stated seeds~\cite{klees2018evaluating,schloegel2024sok}, and IR
evaluation has compared the significance tests available for paired
comparisons~\cite{smucker2007comparison,urbano2019statistical}. We found no differential or metamorphic testing of metadata-filter semantics across vector stores. Ours is
differential in McKeeman's sense: every store's translation is compared with one reference
implementation, not with a rewriting of the same query as in NoREC's metamorphic relation, and it
evaluates its relations under exact search, so that approximation cannot confound them
(Section~\ref{sec:conformance}). We also turn canaries into a deterministic enforcement check over
hits, prompts and answers, report zero-event bounds by the rule of three~\cite{hanley1983if}, and
report confidence intervals over repeated runs.

\paragraph{Industry systems.}
Among native store features, Azure AI Search (in preview) goes furthest: it builds filters from the
caller's Entra token, expands groups through Microsoft Graph at query time, and returns a server error
rather than a partially filtered result when evaluation fails. Yet its document ACLs are only as fresh
as the last synchronization, and earlier preview APIs returned all ACL-protected documents to callers
who used an API key without a user token, until November 2025~\cite{microsoft2026querytime}.
Elasticsearch and OpenSearch enforce document-level security through per-role
queries~\cite{elastic2026controlling,opensearch2026documentlevel}. Elsewhere,
per-document permissions reduce to filters the application supplies: Milvus supports no RBAC at the
partition-key level~\cite{milvus2026implement}, and Qdrant removed payload filters from its
token-based RBAC in version 1.16, citing inconsistent read and write semantics~\cite{qdrant2025release}.
Authorization vendors ship one integration per engine, as a post-filter~\cite{auth02026authorization},
a pre-filter~\cite{cerbos2026implementing} or both~\cite{authzed2026langchainspicedb}; none widens
until $k$ authorized hits arrive or re-checks pre-filtered hits, and OpenFGA acknowledges that
post-filtering may return fewer than $k$ results and suggests over-fetching two to three
times~\cite{openfga2026rag}. Enterprise assistants mirror ACLs into their indexes, so revocation waits
for the next connector synchronization, daily for Coveo's Dynamics 365 provider~\cite{coveo2026coveo}.
The closest commercial analogue to the layer we test is Amazon Bedrock's managed knowledge-base ACL
awareness: it pre-filters on crawled ACLs, verifies each returned document against the data source in
real time for the connectors that support it (not S3 or custom sources), lets denials override grants and withholds documents on transient errors, while stating
that the feature is not a security boundary~\cite{aws2026access}. Open-source projects cover several
stores with metadata only~\cite{langchain2026pebbloretrieval}, or one store with self-reported
tests~\cite{gelevanog2026permissionaware}. We claim no novelty for pre-filtering with a live re-check
and failing closed, which Bedrock ships. We found no independent evaluation of permission
correctness, and no published recall evaluation, for any of these products, and no project that
combines many vector stores with many authorization engines; the latter is an engineering property of
our harness, not a research claim, and our measurements of one layer do not stand in for an evaluation of these products.
