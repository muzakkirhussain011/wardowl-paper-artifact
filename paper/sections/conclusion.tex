Permission-aware retrieval is usually described as a filter. We have argued that it is better
treated as a composition of untrusted parts, translators, approximate indexes and authorization
engines, around one trusted part, the check API. Re-checking every hit with that API makes the
composition secure whatever the other parts do, and our measurements bear this out: no leak in
783,888 checked end-to-end hits over eleven configurations, including red-team prompts, and none in
623,279 requests under injected faults.

Security, however, is the easy half. The other parts still decide how much of what a person may read
they actually receive, and there the stack is far from faithful. One engine's query plans disagreed
with its checks on data of an unexpected shape, stores dropped matching documents at size limits and
one crashed its client on a long filter, and filtered approximate search lost much of a readable set
when that set was of intermediate size or far from the question: at their defaults, five of nine
stores answered filters admitting 0.5\% to 10\% of a million passages worse than smaller ones, and
the same pgvector configuration was exact in some runs and lost a third of its recall in others,
depending on index maintenance no setting names. None of this reached the application as a signal.
It can be made to: a filter-only count flagged every truly short answer without a false alarm, and an
exact search within a bound repaired them; but no count sees a full answer of the wrong chunks, which
was most of the remaining loss, and only a sampled exact search or a better layout addresses it.

Three practices follow. Test filter semantics differentially, against a reference that shares no code
with the translators, and measure recall under permission-shaped filters against exact search over
the readable set, on measured correlation axes, before choosing a store, a layout or a plan. Never let
a system call a short approximate answer complete: count, and fall back to exact search within a
bound, or say that results may be missing; and sample exact searches to see what a count cannot.
Treat the shape and timing of an answer as outputs too: where unreadable chunks near a query must not
be inferable, search exactly and pad latency. We release the kit, the harnesses and the data so that
these checks can be repeated as stores and engines change.
