We measured one permission-aware retrieval layer, developed by the author, as a composition of untrusted parts, translators, approximate indexes and filter APIs, around a
trusted computing base centered on the check API. Outside positive controls built to leak, in what we tested no returned chunk lay outside the readable set at the revision the check read: none in eleven end-to-end
configurations ($8.0 \times 10^{-4}$ per question and $1.6 \times 10^{-2}$ per person, one-sided 95\%, under the independence assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:e2e-safety}) and none in 1,050 fault episodes, beside 40 control episodes that turned denials into allows and leaked, as they must. Those end-to-end runs had no violations, so they do not show the re-check doing anything; end to end it was exercised only when we broke the filter on purpose, on one seed and an exact store, where the leak checks failed and the re-check contained the fault. No adaptive adversary was run; that containment does not depend on the filter or the index is an argument under stated assumptions, not a measurement.

Containment is not all that security asks. The checked revision can be stale, and in the code we ran revoked chunks were served for up to 38.4\,s after an invalidation. In forced-choice tests, latency, counts, recall and scores
told a subject which of two worlds held a group of unreadable chunks that included the query's nearest passages. And the untrusted parts still decide how much of what a subject may
read it receives: on our synthetic layouts stores dropped matching chunks at size limits, filtered approximate search lost much of a readable set far from the
question, and the same pgvector configuration was exact in some runs and lost about a quarter of its recall in others. That loss is not a cost of enforcement as such: in a side run on another corpus, PostgreSQL row-level security kept recall@10 at 0.998 or better, with median latency growing with the readable share to 556\,ms. No count sees a full answer
of the wrong chunks.

On this evidence we suggest three practices. Test filter semantics differentially, and measure recall under permission-shaped filters against exact search over the
readable set before choosing a store, a layout or a plan. Do not let a system call a short approximate answer complete: count, fall back to exact
search within a bound, and sample exact searches. Treat the shape and timing of an answer as outputs too, and the
staleness of a check as part of the claim.
