The flag of Section~\ref{sec:system} stops the layer from calling a short approximate answer complete. Here we decide
cheaply why an answer is short, and repair it within a stated cost.

\paragraph{Design.}
When a pre-filtered search on a backend that does not declare exact search returns $h < k$ hits, the layer asks for
$n = \min(\#\{d : m_d \models \varphi_s\},\ L)$, a filter-only count that stops at a limit $L$. If $n \le h$, the store
returned every chunk the filter admits (\emph{exhausted}); if $n > h$, chunks that pass the filter were missed
(\emph{missed}). After a proven miss the layer can search again exactly under the same filter, when the count is at
most a bound $B$ (10,000 by default); above the bound the result says \emph{over\_limit}. The
alternative \emph{widen} repeats the search with more effort and certifies nothing. Every hit passes the same
re-check, so Observation~\ref{prop:reduction} holds unchanged. A sampled
\emph{monitor} also runs the exact search for a share of requests and reports the overlap of the two top-$k$
lists.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We evaluate the policies on pgvector and Qdrant, on the enterprise scenario (1,468 questions with something to read) and the
6,200 pairs of the MS MARCO layouts, three fresh builds per store and corpus. Seven hypotheses were pre-registered.

\paragraph{Results.}
The count raised no false alarm and flagged every truly short first search as \emph{missed}: 992 of 992 for pgvector on the
enterprise corpus, and 574 of 574 for pgvector and 1,091 of 1,091 for Qdrant on MS MARCO, pooling $k = 5$ and 10 over three builds of the same pairs (H5.1 confirmed).
On unchanging data this follows from what a count is, since a count above $h$ proves a miss, so it checks the stores' counts and the harness and is no discovery; a write between the search and
the count, the case that could make it wrong, was not tested. Repaired exactly
without a bound, no answer was empty or short, and the repaired answers reached recall@10 of 1.0000 (H5.2 confirmed for the
unbounded arm), as exact search should. The bound decides where repair applies: on MS MARCO every short answer of Qdrant had
more than 10,000 readable chunks, so the bounded arms repaired none and reported each as \emph{over\_limit} and not complete. The
cost on queries that did not trigger is inconclusive (H5.3). No repair touched Qdrant's loss on the enterprise corpus (H5.4 confirmed). The 1\% monitor estimated Qdrant's
recall within 0.030 and 0.004 (H5.5 confirmed), so on these corpora a 1\% sample was enough to see the loss that the flag cannot. Exact repair beat
widening by 0.085 of recall@10 (95\% interval 0.064 to 0.110) on the 421 questions that triggered in both arms (H5.6 confirmed).
On the enterprise corpus Qdrant's per-tenant graphs reached recall@10 of 0.939 while the best library arm on the shared layout
reached 0.654 (H5.7 confirmed). Neither count nor repair sees a full answer of the wrong chunks, which was most of the loss on both
stores; only a sampled exact search sees it, and only a better index or layout removes it.
